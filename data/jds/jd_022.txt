About GoGoGrandparent
We built GoGo for our own grandparents and were amazed to see it grow to touch the lives of hundreds of thousands of seniors across the United States and Canada. What shocked us then and it still does now, is that between 30 - 40% of our new signups have smartphones. We didn’t understand why. When we called and asked a few, they told us that Uber and Lyft had 'stopped working' for them.

After puzzling over that for months, we realized what the problem was.

Managing transportation - on Lyft, Uber, cab companies, etc is not easy. It’s a ‘self-serve’ experience. The ride requester has to be aware of things like the driver cancelling, getting lost, the driver arriving, the driver not-quite-being-lost-just-down-the-street-a-couple-houses.

Then add the complexities of smartphones: the user has to be on top of updating their own credit card - making sure the pickup pin is in the correct spot - typing in both where they are and where they’re going. All in all, there are about a dozen online and offline "micro steps" ride requesters have to take to get a ride. And god help anyone who needs to remember their Apple iCloud password to update an app.

We didn't know this when we started, but it turns out that ordering and managing a ride gets more difficult the older you get, almost in the same way that driving a car gets more difficult the older you get.

What gets us excited now (and what we’re hiring for) is that technically this ‘self-management problem’ doesn’t just stop at rides. There are lots of things that get harder to do as you age. People have known this for a long time and that’s inspired a lot of the solutions that older adults currently have.

Based a lot on the experiences we’ve had so far, we believe that by becoming the ‘management layer’ for the things that older adults struggle with as they age we have a better chance to offer older citizens independence without having to ask them to move out of their homes.

Within the next ten years the number of people over the age of 65 will be larger than the number of people under 18 for the first time in history. 11,000,000 people over the age of 75 will stop driving due to age related cognitive or physical decline. When they stop driving, they’ll be forced to rely on friends and family, live in a community, or have caregivers. GoGo is working to make sure that losing your youth doesn’t mean losing your independence.

Joining us at this time is a really exciting opportunity for folks looking to make meaningful impact on people’s lives. We’re a small team that’s built a profitable and fast-growing business over the last three years. Now we’re looking for partners to join us as we make GoGo a household name.

Anyone joining GoGo at this phase of the company will have a huge and life changing level of impact on a size-able and growing user base. We want people to have rich and meaningful lives at any age. We’re bringing people freedom and hope, one life at a time.

About the role
About the Company
GoGoGrandparent’s (GoGo’s) mission is to help older adults live independently in their home for as long as possible. We do that by tailoring existing on demand apps to the needs of people living with mobility, visual, cognitive and dexterity impairments and letting older adults use them by making a phone call. After ordering, our service becomes a virtual caregiver that monitors the instrumental activities of daily living (mobility, meals, groceries, medication delivery & home management) for tens of thousands of older adults around the United States. We’re profitable, have been around since 2016 & haven’t raised any venture capital financing to date. The company was inspired by the cofounder’s grandmother, who still uses us to this day (she’s turning 95 in March)!

Our engineering team is fully remote, tight-knit, and moves fast. You’ll work closely with founders, ship meaningful features weekly, and have real ownership over architecture, reliability, and product direction.

Job Summary
We have a Webflow designer who owns layout and CMS. He does not write JavaScript.

You will own everything that makes our Webflow pages work: registration, payments, service checks, analytics, cookies, and talking to our APIs.

You will also take the code from concatenated ES5/jQuery to TypeScript with a real build pipeline, without breaking production landing pages.

You are not a backend hire. You are expected to read our Node/TypeScript/GraphQL/REST API well enough to decide which endpoint to call, what to send, what comes back, and what happens when it fails. We do not have complete, trustworthy API documentation for this. The source, and its tests, is the documentation.

You need to be comfortable communicating and working directly with our marketing team and other company stakeholders.

What you will actually do
Product / integration

Wire Webflow forms and pages to existing REST and GraphQL endpoints: callers, loved ones, membership, Stripe payment, service-area checks, grocery/home-services leads, error logging.
Own multi-step registration: validation, session persistence, step navigation, auth tokens, 401 handling, partner vs consumer flows.
Keep Stripe Elements, phone inputs, cookies, and tracking working across many landing-page variants.
Coordinate with the Webflow person on DOM IDs, required attributes, and page structure. Our form reader is DOM-driven; a wrong ID on a Webflow page is a production bug.
Take responsibility for our Playwright test coverage and add more tests as needed.
Reading the backend (required, not optional)

Open the relevant route/controller in our backend (TypeScript, Express-style routes, controllers, some GraphQL) and extract: HTTP method, path, auth, request body/query, success payload, error codes and shapes.
Use existing route tests as a second source of truth when the handler is messy.
Choose the right endpoint for the page (create vs patch, REST vs GraphQL, payment intent vs caller update). Calling the wrong one, or omitting a field the controller actually requires, is a failed task.
When the API cannot do what the page needs, write a clear request for backend (expected input/output, current behavior, what's missing). You will not be expected to own large backend changes.
Engineering

Over time, migrate the codebase from JS IIFE modules to TypeScript.
Replace concat + Terser with a real bundler (esbuild/Vite/Rollup).
Untangle globals, jQuery, and implicit script order without a rewrite that takes the site down.
What the systems look like
Client

We use Webflow: a visual web design and development platform that lets you create, build, and host custom websites without writing manual production code, while also supporting custom HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for advanced functionality and customization.
Vanilla JS + jQuery, IIFEs, concatenated in a fixed file order.
Playwright tests already use TypeScript. App code does not.
API

Large TypeScript Node service. Registration and billing are not a tiny CRUD layer; controllers have real business rules.
Auth is typically bearer tokens; some flows differ (partner vs consumer).
Error responses are not always uniform. You have to read the handler.
